**Simple Summary**
The Fallacy of Composition incorrectly projects properties of individual parts onto the entire whole. 
The Fallacy of Division incorrectly projects properties of the whole onto its individual parts.

**Core Structure & Mechanics**
* **Fallacy of Composition:**
  * **Mechanism:** Part $p$ of Whole $W$ has property $X \rightarrow$ Conclude Whole $W$ as a total unit must have property $X$.
  * **Key Red Flag:** Generalizing that a system or group behaves identically to its individual components.
* **Fallacy of Division:**
  * **Mechanism:** Whole $W$ has property $X \rightarrow$ Conclude individual Part $p$ within $W$ must have property $X$.
  * **Key Red Flag:** Assuming an individual inherits the macro-level attributes, success, or status of the organization or collective they belong to.

**Key Thinkers & Sources**
* **Associated Thinkers:** [[Aristotle]] (Identified both in *Sophistical Refutations*), [[Bertrand Russell]] (Explored composition in set theory and logic)
* **Primary Text:** *[[Sophistical Refutations]]* by [[Aristotle]]

* **Apologetics & Philosophy Application:** Spotting when critics or apologists confuse emergent properties of a complex system (like consciousness or cosmological models) with the simple properties of its subatomic constituent parts.

**Common Misconceptions**
* Reasoning from parts to a whole is not fallacious if the property in question is additive or invariant across scales (e.g., "Every part of this statue is made of bronze, therefore the entire statue is made of bronze").
* The Fallacy of Division is distinct from Ad Hominem; it deals with part/whole logic errors rather than direct personal attacks.
