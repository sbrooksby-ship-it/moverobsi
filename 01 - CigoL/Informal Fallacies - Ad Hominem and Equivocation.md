**Simple Summary**
An Ad Hominem attempts to discredit an argument by attacking the person  rather than the claim itself. 
Equivocation relies on shifting the definition of a single key term mid-argument to force a false conclusion.

**Core Structure & Mechanics**
* **Ad Hominem (*Argumentum ad Hominem*):**
  * **Mechanism:** Person $X$ makes Claim $A \rightarrow$ Attack $X$'s character $\rightarrow$ Reject $A$ as false.
  * **Key Red Flag:** Focus shifts away from facts/data toward personal insults, hypocrisy charges (*tu quoque*), or ulterior motives.
* **Equivocation:**
  * **Mechanism:** Premise uses Term $T$ with Meaning $M_1 \rightarrow$ Conclusion uses Term $T$ with Meaning $M_2$.
  * **Key Red Flag:** A word with multiple meanings appears in both the premises and the conclusion, but the logic only works if you swap definitions.

**Key Thinkers & Sources**
* **Associated Thinkers:** [[Aristotle]] (Identified semantic shifts in *Sophistical Refutations*)
* **Primary Text:** *[[Attacking Faulty Reasoning]]* by [[T. Edward Damer]]

**Connections & Links**
* **Parent Category:** [[01 - Logic]]
* **Prerequisite Concepts:** [[Informal Fallacies - Strawman and Begging the Question]], [[Validity and Soundness]]
* **Related Fallacies:** [[Genetic Fallacy]], [[Red Herring]], [[Amphiboly]]
* **Apologetics Application:** Identifying when critics dismiss an argument simply because of the person giving it ([[Ad Hominem]]) or when key theological terms like "faith" or "freedom" shift definitions mid-debate ([[Equivocation]]).

**Common Misconceptions**
* Simply insulting someone isn't automatically an Ad Hominem. It is only a fallacy if the insult is used *as the reason* to dismiss their argument.
* Pointing out genuine hypocritical behavior is not an Ad Hominem if the argument being debated is specifically about that person's personal credibility or integrity.
