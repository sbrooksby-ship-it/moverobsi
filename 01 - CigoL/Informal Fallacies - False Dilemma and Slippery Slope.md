**Simple Summary**
A False Dilemma restricts a situation to two extreme choices while ignoring valid middle options. 

A Slippery Slope claims without sufficient evidence that a minor first step will inevitably trigger an extreme chain reaction.

**Core Structure & Mechanics**
* **False Dilemma (False Dichotomy):**
  * **Mechanism:** Frame $A$ vs $B$ as the only options $\rightarrow$ Reject $B \rightarrow$ Force acceptance of $A$ (ignoring $C, D, \text{or } E$).
  * **Key Red Flag:** Language like "You're either with us or against us" or reducing complex issues to binary choices.
* **Slippery Slope:**
  * **Mechanism:** Step $X \rightarrow$ Unsubstantiated chain ($Y_1 \rightarrow Y_2 \rightarrow \dots$) $\rightarrow$ Extreme Disaster $Z$.
  * **Key Red Flag:** Escalating predictions of total collapse based on a small policy tweak, lacking proven causal links.

**Key Thinkers & Sources**
* **Associated Thinkers:** [[Aristotle]] (Cataloged disjunctive logic traps in *Prior Analytics*)
* **Primary Text:** *[[Asking the Right Questions: A Guide to Critical Thinking]]* by [[M. Neil Browne]] & [[Stuart M. Keeley]]

**Common Misconceptions**
* Presenting two options isn't a fallacy if there genuinely *are* only two exhaustive options (e.g., "Either the light switch is on or off").
* Predicting a series of future consequences is not a Slippery Slope if each step in the causal chain is backed by historical data or clear evidence.
