**Simple Summary**
An Appeal to Authority claims something is true solely because a figure of status endorsed it. 
The Bandwagon Fallacy claims an idea is true or right because it is popular or widely accepted.

**Core Structure & Mechanics**
* **Appeal to Authority (*Argumentum ad Verecundiam*):**
  * **Mechanism:** Person $A$ claims $P \rightarrow$ Person $A$ is an authority figure $\rightarrow$ Therefore, $P$ is true.
  * **Key Red Flag:** Relying on titles, celebrity status, or out-of-field credentials instead of verifiable arguments and data.
* **Bandwagon Fallacy (*Argumentum ad Populum*):**
  * **Mechanism:** Most people believe or do $P \rightarrow$ Therefore, $P$ is true or correct.
  * **Key Red Flag:** Using headcount, vote totals, or market share to prove a factual or moral claim.

**Key Thinkers & Sources**
* **Associated Thinkers:** [[John Locke]] (Coined *argumentum ad verecundiam* in *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding*), [[Socrates]]
* **Primary Text:** *[[An Essay Concerning Human Understanding]]* by [[John Locke]]

**Connections & Links**

* **Apologetics Application:** Spotting when critics reject a position purely because "society has moved past it" ([[Bandwagon Fallacy]]) or citing a high-profile figure who holds an opinion outside their domain of expertise ([[Appeal to Authority]]).

**Common Misconceptions**
* Citing relevant expert consensus backed by empirical evidence is valid reasoning; it becomes a fallacy when authority replaces actual evidence or comes from an irrelevant field.
* Popular consensus can dictate cultural norms, but sheer numbers never establish objective truth.
