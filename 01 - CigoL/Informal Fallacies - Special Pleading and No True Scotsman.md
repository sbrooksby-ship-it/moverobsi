**Simple Summary**
Special Pleading applying a rule, standard, or principle to others while making an unjustified exception for yourself or your own argument without providing a valid reason.
No True Scotsman protects a universal claim from counterexamples by arbitrarily modifying criteria on the spot.

**Core Structure & Mechanics**
* **Special Pleading:**
  * **Mechanism:** Rule $R$ applies to Category $C \rightarrow$ Object $X$ belongs to Category $C \rightarrow$ Object $X$ claims an exemption from $R$ with no logical justification.
  * **Key Red Flag:** Demanding double standards or claiming one's position is "beyond standard rules of evidence."
* **No True Scotsman (Ad Hoc Redefinition):**
  * **Mechanism:** Claim "All $A$ are $B$" $\rightarrow$ Counterexample presented ($A_1$ is an $A$, but not $B$) $\rightarrow$ Modify claim: "No *true* $A$ is not $B$."
  * **Key Red Flag:** Adding unwritten purity tests to a definition after being presented with a disproving example.

**Key Thinkers & Sources**
* **Associated Thinkers:** [[Antony Flew]] (Coined "No True Scotsman" in *Thinking About Thinking*)
* **Primary Text:** *[[Thinking About Thinking]]* by [[Antony Flew]]

**Common Misconceptions**
* Modifying a definition isn't fallacious if you explicitly state precise criteria beforehand rather than changing them reactively to dodge a counterexample.
* Pointing out a genuine, pre-existing boundary or requirement for a group (e.g., "A vegetarian by definition does not eat meat") is not a No True Scotsman fallacy.
