**Simple Summary**
A Hasty Generalization reaches a broad conclusion using an inadequate sample size. 

Post Hoc Ergo Propter Hoc mistakes correlation or sequential timing for a direct cause-and-effect relationship.

**Core Structure & Mechanics**
* **Hasty Generalization (*Secundum Quid*):**
  * **Mechanism:** Sample $S$ (small/biased) has Property $P \rightarrow$ Conclude entire Population $N$ has Property $P$.
  * **Key Red Flag:** Sweeping statements ("Everyone", "Never", "All of them") backed by a single personal anecdote or tiny sample.
* **Post Hoc Ergo Propter Hoc:**
  * **Mechanism:** Event $A$ occurs before Event $B \rightarrow$ Conclude $A$ directly caused $B$.
  * **Key Red Flag:** Assuming sequence equals causation without demonstrating a plausible physical or logical mechanism.

**Key Thinkers & Sources**
* **Associated Thinkers:** [[David Hume]] (Critiqued human assumptions about necessary causation and induction), [[Aristotle]]
* **Primary Text:** *[[An Introduction to Logic and Scientific Method]]* by [[Morris R. Cohen]] and [[Ernest Nagel]]

**Connections & Links**
* **Parent Category:** [[01 - Logic]]
* **Prerequisite Concepts:** [[Inductive Reasoning]], [[Correlation vs Causation]], [[Validity and Soundness]]
* **Related Fallacies:** [[Confirmation Bias]], [[Texas Sharpshooter Fallacy]], [[Slippery Slope]]
* **Apologetics Application:** Spotting when critics reject broad historical claims based on isolated negative experiences ([[Hasty Generalization]]) or when coincidences are incorrectly cited as definitive proof/disproof without establishing causal links ([[Post Hoc]]).

**Common Misconceptions**
* Drawing a conclusion from a small sample is valid if the sample is fully representative and statistically rigorous.
* Just because Event A happening before Event B doesn't *prove* causation doesn't mean they are unrelated; it simply means temporal order alone isn't sufficient proof.
