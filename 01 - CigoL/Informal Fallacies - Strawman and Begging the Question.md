**Simple Summary**
Informal fallacies are errors in content rather than pure form. A Strawman exaggerates an opponent's point to make it easier to attack because it is never claimed 
Begging the Question assumes the conclusion as a premise, creating circular reasoning.

**Core Structure & Mechanics**
* **Strawman Fallacy:**
  * **Mechanism:** Claim $A \rightarrow$ Distorted to Claim $A^* \rightarrow$ Attack $A^*$ as if it were $A$.
  * **Key Red Flag:** "So what you're saying is..." followed by an extreme exaggeration.
* **Begging the Question (*Petitio Principii*):**
  * **Mechanism:** Premise $P \implies C$, where $P$ relies on assuming $C$ is already true.
  * **Key Red Flag:** Circular justification where no external evidence is actually added.

**Key Thinkers & Sources**
* **Associated Thinkers:** [[Aristotle]] (Cataloged fallacies in *Sophistical Refutations*)
* **Primary Text:** *[[Socratic Logic]]* by [[Peter Kreeft]]

**Common Misconceptions**
* "Begging the question" does not mean "raises the question." In logic, it strictly means circular reasoning.
* An argument containing a fallacy doesn't automatically mean the final conclusion is false—it just means the argument presented didn't actually prove it (known as the *Fallacy Fallacy*).
