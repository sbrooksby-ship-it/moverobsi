**Simple Summary**
A Genetic Fallacy judges an argument based on where it came from rather than its evidence. 
An Appeal to Ignorance claims a statement is true simply because it hasn't been disproven (or vice versa).

**Core Structure & Mechanics**
* **Genetic Fallacy:**
  * **Mechanism:** Claim $C$ originates from Source $S \rightarrow$ Source $S$ has History/Origin $H \rightarrow$ Claim $C$ is rejected or accepted based on $H$ rather than evidence.
  * **Key Red Flag:** Dismissing an idea immediately by pointing to its historical or cultural origin without evaluating the argument itself.
* **Appeal to Ignorance (*Argumentum ad Ignorantiam*):**
  * **Mechanism:** Statement $P$ has not been proven false $\rightarrow$ Therefore, $P$ is true. (OR: $P$ has not been proven true $\rightarrow$ Therefore, $P$ is false.)
  * **Key Red Flag:** Shifting the burden of proof onto the listener to "disprove" an unevidenced claim.

**Key Thinkers & Sources**
* **Associated Thinkers:** [[Morris Cohen]] and [[Ernest Nagel]] (Popularized the term "genetic fallacy" in logic textbooks), [[Carl Sagan]] (Discussed burden of proof and claims in *The Demon-Haunted World*)
* **Primary Text:** *[[Logick: Or, the Right Use of Reason in the Enquiry After Truth]]* by [[Isaac Watts]]

* **Apologetics Application:** Recognizing when critics dismiss early historical documents solely because of their religious origins ([[Genetic Fallacy]]), or identifying when arguments for or against a transcendent cause rely on gaps in current scientific knowledge rather than positive evidence ([[Appeal to Ignorance]]).

**Common Misconceptions**
* Investigating the history or context of an argument is useful historical analysis; it only becomes a Genetic Fallacy if origin is used as the sole reason to invalidate the argument's logic.
* Pointing out a lack of evidence is standard critical thinking, but concluding that the opposite *must be true* as a result is where the fallacy occurs.
